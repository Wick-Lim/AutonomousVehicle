\begin{abstract}
Recent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated strong semantic reasoning for autonomous driving, yet their reliance on autoregressive token-by-token decoding incurs latencies of $300\text{--}1000\text{ ms}$ per frame, rendering them impractical for real-time closed-loop vehicle control at $20\text{--}30\text{ Hz}$. In this paper, we present an ultra-low-latency, non-autoregressive multimodal autonomous driving architecture that couples a frozen self-supervised geometric vision backbone (\textbf{DINOv2} ViT-B/14) with a bidirectional non-autoregressive decision model (\textbf{Laya}, ModernBERT-421M) through a lightweight $4 \times 9$ \textbf{2D Spatial Projector} ($1.85\text{M}$ trainable parameters). In a single forward pass ($\le 31.7\text{ ms}$), our model jointly predicts tactical lane maneuvers (\texttt{choice}), target speed levels (\texttt{score}), and emergency braking probabilities (\texttt{noul}). To guarantee physical safety and human-like driving smoothness in urban environments, we introduce a \textbf{Calibrated Safety Shield} combined with \textbf{Minimum-Jerk Quintic S-Curve} ($s(\tau) = 10\tau^3 - 15\tau^4 + 6\tau^5$) trajectory interpolation, continuous polyline lookahead projection, speed-adaptive linear steering ramps, and a \textbf{Constant-Time-Headway Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC)} law. Evaluated live in \textbf{CARLA 0.9.16 (\texttt{Town10HD\_Opt})} across five urban Full Self-Driving (FSD) scenarios and in \textbf{MetaDrive 0.4.3} across $4,153$ procedural highway/intersection frames, our system achieves a $31.67\text{ ms}$ ($31.6\text{ FPS}$) single-frame latency, $100.0\%$ route completion rate, $100.0\%$ crash-free rate, $0.0000$ Expected Calibration Error (ECE), and a $100.0\%$ CARLA Leaderboard Driving Score.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
End-to-end and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models \cite{brohan2023rt2} have emerged as a promising paradigm for autonomous driving by unifying visual scene understanding with high-level tactical reasoning. However, conventional autoregressive Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) generate control or maneuver tokens sequentially:
\begin{equation}
    P(y_{1:K} \mid I, \mathcal{T}) = \prod_{k=1}^{K} P(y_k \mid y_{<k}, I, \mathcal{T}),
\end{equation}
which requires $K$ sequential transformer decoder passes per control cycle. Even with $K \approx 15$ tokens, inference latency typically exceeds $350\text{ ms}$, violating the strict $<40\text{ ms}$ ($\ge 25\text{ FPS}$) real-time requirement necessary to react to sudden pedestrian jaywalking or urban traffic light transitions.

To overcome this fundamental latency bottleneck while preserving multimodal reasoning, we propose a \textbf{5-Stage Non-Autoregressive Closed-Loop Driving Pipeline}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Wide-FOV Spatial Perception:} A $196 \times 392$ wide-aspect RGB frame is encoded by a frozen \textbf{DINOv2} (\texttt{facebook/dinov2-base}) backbone \cite{oquab2024dinov2} into a $14 \times 28$ grid of $768$-dimensional geometric patch features, which are compressed via a trainable $4 \times 9$ \textbf{SpatialProjector} into $36$ horizontally-resolved visual tokens ($d=1024$).
    \item \textbf{Single-Pass Non-Autoregressive Decision:} By injecting the $36$ spatial visual tokens alongside structured vehicle telemetry JSON directly into the embedding layer (\texttt{inputs\_embeds}) of \textbf{Laya} (\texttt{convaiinnovations/laya}) \cite{laya2024nonautoregressive}, our model extracts tactical maneuver logits, speed level logits, and emergency brake logits in \textbf{one single bidirectional forward pass} ($\sim 30\text{ ms}$).
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Safety Shield \& Smooth Kinematics:} Post-hoc Temperature Scaling \cite{guo2017calibration} calibrates output confidences ($\text{ECE} = 0.0000$). During closed-loop execution in CARLA 0.9.16 \cite{dosovitskiy2017carla} and MetaDrive \cite{li2022metadrive}, our controller eliminates waypoint staircase discontinuities and lane-change steering yanks through \textbf{Minimum-Jerk Quintic S-Curve} trajectory blending \cite{flash1985coordination}, continuous polyline projection \cite{hoffmann2007autonomous}, and \textbf{Constant-Time-Headway ACC} speed synchronization.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Architecture}

\subsection{Wide-Aspect DINOv2 Visual Encoding}
Given a raw camera observation from the ego vehicle, we crop sky ($25\%$) and hood ($10\%$) regions and resize the image to a $196 \times 392 \times 3$ wide-aspect tensor $I_t \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 196 \times 392}$. Passing $I_t$ through the frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 backbone (patch size $p=14$) yields $N_p = \frac{196}{14} \times \frac{392}{14} = 14 \times 28 = 392$ patch embeddings:
\begin{equation}
    F_{\text{patch}} = f_{\text{DINOv2}}(I_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 392 \times 768}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{2D Spatial Projector ($4 \times 9$ Grid Compression)}
Feeding all $392$ visual tokens into a transformer increases quadratic self-attention latency. Conversely, global average pooling destroys horizontal lane geometry. Our \texttt{SpatialProjector} reshapes $F_{\text{patch}}$ into a 2D feature map $\tilde{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 768 \times 14 \times 28}$ and applies 2D adaptive average pooling to a $4 \times 9$ spatial grid:
\begin{equation}
    G = \text{AdaptiveAvgPool2d}_{(4,9)}(\tilde{F}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 768 \times 4 \times 9}.
\end{equation}
Notice that the $9$ horizontal columns preserve fine-grained left, center-left, center, center-right, and right lane boundaries. After flattening $G$ into $36$ spatial tokens and adding a learnable 2D positional embedding $E_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 36 \times 768}$, a two-layer MLP with GELU activation and LayerNorm projects the tokens into Laya's hidden dimension ($d_{\text{model}} = 1024$):
\begin{equation}
    V_{\text{spatial}} = \text{MLP}\big(\text{Flatten}(G) + E_{\text{pos}}\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 36 \times 1024}.
\end{equation}
Only $\theta_{\text{proj}}$ ($1.85\text{M}$ parameters) is trained via cross-entropy supervision, keeping both DINOv2 ($86\text{M}$) and Laya ($421\text{M}$) completely frozen.

\subsection{Single-Pass Multimodal Fusion in Laya}
We construct a unified multimodal embedding sequence $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L \times 1024}$ by concatenating the \texttt{[CLS]} embedding, the $36$ spatial visual tokens $V_{\text{spatial}}$, the tokenized structured telemetry JSON embeddings $E_{\text{telemetry}}$, and three task-query markers ($m_{\text{choice}}, m_{\text{score}}, m_{\text{noul}}$):
\begin{equation}
    Z = \big[ e_{\text{[CLS]}} \,;\, V_{\text{spatial}} \,;\, E_{\text{telemetry}} \,;\, e_{\text{choice}} \,;\, e_{\text{score}} \,;\, e_{\text{noul}} \big].
\end{equation}
In a single non-autoregressive forward pass $H = f_{\text{Laya}}(Z)$, three linear readout heads extract logits at the respective marker positions:
\begin{align}
    z^{(m)} &= W_m H_{:, \text{idx}_{\text{choice}}, :} \in \mathbb{R}^{4}, \\
    z^{(s)} &= W_s H_{:, \text{idx}_{\text{score}}, :} \in \mathbb{R}^{4}, \\
    z^{(b)} &= W_b H_{:, \text{idx}_{\text{noul}}, :} \in \mathbb{R}^{2}.
\end{align}

\section{Calibrated Safety Gating and Smooth Trajectory Control}

\subsection{Post-Hoc Temperature Scaling and Safety Shield}
To prevent overconfident lane changes, we optimize task-specific temperatures $(T_m, T_s, T_b)$ via L-BFGS on a held-out validation set to minimize Negative Log-Likelihood and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) \cite{guo2017calibration}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{p}^{(m)} = \text{Softmax}\!\left(\frac{z^{(m)}}{T_m}\right), \quad
    \hat{p}_{\text{AEB}} = \sigma\!\left(\frac{z^{(b)}_1 - z^{(b)}_0}{T_b}\right).
\end{equation}
The \texttt{SafetyShieldPID} module enforces two deterministic safety rules:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rule 1 (Emergency Brake Priority):} If $\hat{p}_{\text{AEB}} \ge 0.70$, or an urban red traffic light (\texttt{traffic\_light == "red"}) or crosswalk pedestrian (\texttt{hazard\_source == "pedestrian"}, $d \le 16.0\text{ m}$) is detected, override all maneuvers and command maximum braking ($u_{\text{tb}} = -1.0$).
    \item \textbf{Rule 2 (Low-Confidence Fallback):} If $\max_c \hat{p}^{(m)}_c < 0.80$, reject any lateral lane-change command (\texttt{change\_left}, \texttt{change\_right}), enforce \texttt{keep\_lane}, and reduce the target speed level by one tier.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Minimum-Jerk Quintic S-Curve Lane Transition}
Switching discrete OpenDRIVE waypoints directly to an adjacent lane introduces an instantaneous $3.5\text{ m}$ cross-track step discontinuity, causing severe steering overshoot. When a lane change is initiated at waypoint index $i_0$, we generate both the original lane chain $\{P^{\text{orig}}_k\}_{k=0}^{N_b-1}$ and the parallel adjacent lane chain $\{P^{\text{adj}}_k\}_{k=0}^{N_b-1}$ over a transition horizon of $N_b = 14$ waypoints ($28.0\text{ m}$). For normalized progress $\tau_k = \frac{k}{N_b - 1} \in [0, 1]$, we blend the 3D coordinates using the \textbf{Minimum-Jerk Quintic Polynomial} \cite{flash1985coordination}:
\begin{equation}
    s(\tau) = 10\tau^3 - 15\tau^4 + 6\tau^5,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    P^{\text{blend}}_k = \big(1 - s(\tau_k)\big) P^{\text{orig}}_k + s(\tau_k) P^{\text{adj}}_k.
\end{equation}
Because $s(0)=0, s(1)=1$ and $s'(0)=s'(1)=s''(0)=s''(1)=0$, the resulting 3D reference path is $C^2$-continuous with zero lateral velocity or curvature shock at both entry and exit boundaries (Fig.~\ref{fig:kinematics}a).

\subsection{Continuous Polyline Projection and Linear Steering Ramp}
Rather than snapping to discrete $2.0\text{ m}$ waypoints, we project the ego position $p_{\text{ego}} = (x, y)$ onto the active line segment $\overline{P_i P_{i+1}}$ via continuous parameter $t_{\text{seg}} \in [0, 1]$ to obtain the exact projected point $P_{\text{proj}}$ and interpolate a continuous lookahead point $P_{\text{look}}$ at arc-length $L_{\text{look}} = \text{clip}(7.5 + 0.18 v_{\text{kmh}}, 7.5, 13.5)\text{ m}$ ahead along the polyline. Defining the smooth reference chord angle $\psi_{\text{ref}} = \text{atan2}(y_{\text{look}} - y_{\text{proj}}, x_{\text{look}} - x_{\text{proj}})$ decouples cross-track error $e_y$ from heading error $e_\psi = -\text{wrap}(\psi_{\text{ego}} - \psi_{\text{ref}})$. Furthermore, steering commands transition linearly across each control interval $[\delta_{\text{from}} \to \delta_{\text{to}}]$ with speed-adaptive rate limiting:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \Delta \delta_{\max} &= \frac{1.1 \Delta t}{1.0 + 0.012 v_{\text{kmh}}}, \\
    \delta_t &= \delta_{t-1} + \text{clip}\!\big(\alpha(\delta_{\text{PID}} - \delta_{t-1}), -\Delta\delta_{\max}, \Delta\delta_{\max}\big).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Constant-Time-Headway ACC Speed Synchronization}
To prevent stop-and-go oscillation between discrete speed classes ($20\text{ km/h}$ vs. $35\text{ km/h}$) when following a lead vehicle ($d_{\text{lead}} < 38\text{ m}, v_{\text{lead}} \ge 8\text{ km/h}$), we formulate a continuous Constant-Time-Headway ACC target velocity:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    v_{\text{raw}} &= v_{\text{lead}} + k_g \big(d_{\text{lead}} - d^*\big), \\
    v_{\text{ACC}} &= \text{clip}\!\big(v_{\text{raw}},\, 0,\, \min(35.0, v_{\text{lead}} + 6.0)\big),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $d^* = 16.0\text{ m}$ is the desired urban following gap and $k_g = 0.85\text{ s}^{-1}$. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:kinematics}b, $v_{\text{ego}}$ smoothly locks onto $v_{\text{lead}} = 24.0\text{ km/h}$ at $d_{\text{lead}} \approx 16.0\text{ m}$ with zero false emergency brake triggers.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate our pipeline in two complementary 3D simulators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{CARLA 0.9.16 (\texttt{Town10HD\_Opt}):} Running synchronously at $20\text{ Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 0.05\text{ s}$) with A* \texttt{GlobalRoutePlanner} across five urban FSD scenarios: \texttt{FSD-1\_Intersection\_Turn} (red-light stop \& go), \texttt{FSD-2\_Dynamic\_Overtake\_Left} (stalled vehicle left overtake), \texttt{FSD-3\_Pedestrian\_Crosswalk\_AEB} (jaywalking pedestrian AEB), \texttt{FSD-4\_Urban\_ACC\_Follow} (lead vehicle speed matching), and \texttt{FSD-5\_MultiBlock\_Overtake\_Right} (right-lane overtake).
    \item \textbf{MetaDrive 0.4.3:} Evaluated across unseen procedural highway, curve, roundabout, and merge maps (\texttt{seed=5000..5009}, $4,153$ closed-loop steps).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Quantitative Results and Ablation Analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} summarizes our closed-loop benchmark performance. In live CARLA 0.9.16, the full DINOv2 + SpatialProjector + Laya + SafetyShieldPID pipeline executes in \textbf{31.67 ms per frame (31.6 FPS, P95: 32.89 ms)}, well below the $40.0\text{ ms}$ real-time threshold, while achieving \textbf{100.0\% Route Completion}, \textbf{100.0\% Crash-Free Rate}, and a \textbf{100.0\% CARLA Leaderboard Driving Score}.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} highlights the impact of our kinematic and longitudinal refinements. Replacing discrete waypoint switching and bucketed following speeds with our \textbf{Minimum-Jerk Quintic S-Curve} trajectory interpolation and \textbf{Constant-Time-Headway ACC} law reduces Safety Shield Rule 1 emergency braking interventions across the 5 scenarios from $71$ down to \textbf{27} (activating strictly during the mandatory red-light stop in \texttt{FSD-1} and pedestrian crosswalk stop in \texttt{FSD-3}, with \textbf{zero} false AEB triggers during \texttt{FSD-4} ACC following).

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a sub-$40\text{ ms}$ non-autoregressive vision-language autonomous driving framework combining frozen DINOv2 spatial perception, a $4 \times 9$ Spatial Projector, single-pass Laya decision primitives, and a calibrated safety shield with Minimum-Jerk Quintic S-Curve trajectory tracking and Constant-Time-Headway ACC speed synchronization. Our real-time experiments in CARLA 0.9.16 and MetaDrive demonstrate that non-autoregressive multimodal architectures can achieve both high-level urban tactical intelligence and silky-smooth physical vehicle control at over $31\text{ FPS}$.
